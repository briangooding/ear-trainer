What the ranges sound like

    The big number is the loudest frequency. Voices and instruments also make overtones at 2×, 3×… the pitch, so you’ll see a comb of peaks; the tallest one isn’t always the note you hear. A whistle or a sine tone gives one clean peak. Tap or drag on the spectrum to read any frequency.

    dB

    Phone microphones aren’t calibrated, so readings are an estimate (often within ±5 dB after calibrating). dBA weights frequencies the way ears hear them and is what hearing-safety limits use; dBZ counts every frequency equally, so bass rumble shows up. Fast follows changes within ⅛ s, Slow averages over 1 s. Only the microphone is measured; a tone from this app counts only as much as the mic hears it from the speaker.

    Use it to learn what 40, 60 and 80 dB feel like, and to keep practice and listening levels comfortable. Around 85 dBA, damage builds up over a working day; each +3 dB halves the safe time.
