What the ranges sound like

    The big number is the loudest frequency. Voices and instruments also make overtones at 2×, 3×… the pitch, so you’ll see a comb of peaks; the tallest one isn’t always the note you hear. A whistle or a sine tone gives one clean peak. Tap or drag on the spectrum to read any frequency.

    Most phone speakers can’t reproduce much below ~150 Hz; use headphones to hear low tones. Keep the volume low, especially with headphones.

    Try the same note as sine vs. square: the spectrum shows the extra overtones that change the tone color.

    Start in Learn: tap each button, listen, and watch where it lands on the spectrum. Then switch to Test. Pure tones are used; the analyzer is hidden until you answer so it can’t give the answer away. Octave landmarks: 125 is a low hum, 1k is a telephone-ish “honk”, 4k is where ears are most sensitive.

    Everything runs on your phone; no audio leaves the device.
